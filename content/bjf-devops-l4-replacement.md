# BJF — DevOps Engineer L4 (Phil Stevenson replacement)

## Justification for Role

The DevOps team comprises four engineers supporting the entire engineering organisation.  The team's mandate is to build tooling that enables engineering productivity and reduces cognitive load, but a growing reactive support burden has increasingly displaced that work.  Maintaining headcount is a prerequisite for addressing that drift.  This requires a senior engineer who will proactively identify and drive improvements to how engineering works.  Operationally, the team owns the observability platform migration (a cross-functional initiative involving Platform, Security, Engineering, and Data) and will need to support upcoming AI infrastructure build-outs.  Reactive support already consumes one engineer full-time on rotation, and that burden is growing as AI-driven developer productivity increases the volume of support requests.

## Risk of not hiring for this role?

Losing one of four engineers increases each remaining engineer's share of reactive support from 25% to 33%, significantly reducing capacity for project delivery.  The team already spent a year delivering the ephemeral environments platform partly because project work was constantly deprioritised for support — at three people, the ability to deliver further projects is seriously compromised.  The observability migration and the DevOps-to-DevEx shift both stall.  More broadly, the rest of engineering can only scale effectively if supported with good tooling; not replacing constrains the organisation's ability to scale, not just the team's.  There is also a retention risk: remaining engineers locked in a cycle of reactive support with diminishing capacity for meaningful project work are likely to disengage or leave.

## Alternatives to recruitment considered?

System optimisation through automation is the primary lever for reducing the reactive support burden, but building that automation requires the capacity this hire would provide — it cannot be achieved with a reduced team.  An internal engineer can step into the technical leadership gap, but this recovers seniority, not headcount.  Streamlining or deprioritising work is not viable: the team's output is split between reactive support (which cannot be dropped) and strategic projects (which should not be dropped), with no discretionary margin.

## Does this role fulfil a strategic goal or critical OKR?

The observability platform migration is a significant strategic initiative with stakeholders across Platform, Security, Engineering, and Data.  Additionally, the SVP of Engineering has identified a need to shift the team's operating model from reactive support toward enabling engineering productivity through automation and tooling.  Both depend on having senior capacity in this team.
